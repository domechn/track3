# Cost basis report

Track3 calculates a **USD estimate** from locally stored transactions. The transaction table records changes between portfolio snapshots, not exchange fills. Its `price` is the snapshot price; it may differ from the execution price. The table has no fee, swap, airdrop, transaction hash, or transfer identifier columns. Review the report against exchange and wallet records before using it for tax filing.

## Accounting rules

- Inventory is grouped by asset type and symbol across the user's wallets. FIFO uses the oldest lot, LIFO the newest, and HIFO the highest cost per unit. Weighted average pools acquisition cost across open lots; it still consumes the oldest dated quantities when determining holding term. Partial sales divide cost, proceeds, and sale fees proportionally.
- A buy adds `quantity × USD price + USD fee` to basis. A sale has `quantity × USD price − USD fee` of net proceeds. Gain or loss is net proceeds less consumed basis. The engine accepts optional nonnegative USD fees, but stored Track3 transactions have no fee field, so the app report cannot include them.
- The pure engine accepts explicit `swap-out` and `swap-in` legs. The outbound leg realizes a disposal and the inbound leg creates a lot at its supplied USD fair value. Stored balance changes are **not** automatically classified as swaps because a snapshot UUID identifies a refresh batch, not a trade.
- The engine accepts explicit `airdrop` events as zero-cost lots. An airdrop's income treatment and fair-value basis depend on jurisdiction; stored deposits cannot be identified as airdrops and are instead flagged with unknown basis.
- Stablecoins are accounted for like other assets, by symbol, using recorded prices. The configured stablecoin list (configuration ID `996`) does not override cost basis or force a $1 peg: a depeg can produce a real gain or loss. No tax exemption is assumed. If the app groups several stablecoin symbols into one, the stored identity may be insufficient to reconstruct original lots.
- Paired opposite changes of the same asset within a snapshot UUID, across different wallets, are treated as transfers when the received quantity is no greater than the sent quantity and the difference is at most 1% of the sent quantity. A matching `withdraw` and `deposit` pair across wallets within seven days is also treated as a transfer. The difference is removed from lot inventory as a network fee, without a sale. Transfers retain the acquisition date and remaining basis. This match is an inference and can confuse unrelated trades; other opposite-wallet sell/buy pairs in one snapshot, or a matching sell/buy on the next asset refresh, get a possible-transfer warning. Explicit `transfer-in` and `transfer-out` engine events need matching transfer IDs; unmatched legs are flagged.
- Unpaired stored `deposit` and `withdraw` events have unknown basis or treatment and are flagged. Deposits do not create assumed zero-cost lots. Withdrawals remove quantity and associated basis from open lots without a realized sale. If a later sale exceeds known lots, the uncovered quantity is flagged as a data gap. The engine reports gains only on covered quantity and never creates negative inventory.
- Holding term is `long` when UTC elapsed time from acquisition to disposal is more than the chosen day threshold (365 by default), otherwise `short`. This is a fixed elapsed-day rule, not the US calendar-year rule: a lot bought 2024-01-01 and sold 2025-01-01 is marked long here because the leap year spans 366 days, while US one-year treatment would mark it short. Review the term before filing.

The tax year and date range filter select disposals and dated warnings using the user's local calendar dates. Invalid events with no usable date remain visible as warnings in every range. Earlier transactions remain in the ledger to supply their basis. Open lots are calculated as of the filter's end date; unrealized P&L uses the latest locally stored asset price, which can be newer than that date. Missing local prices leave unrealized P&L unavailable for the asset.

CSV exports one row per consumed lot, with asset, acquired date, disposed date, quantity, proceeds, basis, gain/loss, term, and method. CSV quantities are rounded to eight decimal places and USD values to cents. JSON includes the full-precision rows, totals, range, and warnings. Both exports use the displayed date range; the assistant also provides converted display-currency totals. No database migration is required: the selected method uses a separate configuration row, included in configuration exports.
